Investment portfolios, risk and costs

Who decides which investment portfolios I use?

You choose your own investment portfolio for both Phase 1 – Wealth Building – and Phase 2 – Wealth Consumption. Neptoon provides a selection of model portfolios with different risk and return profiles, but does not automatically select an investment strategy for you. Each of your two portfolio selections is an independent assumption in the Life Cycle calculation. You can choose the same portfolio for both phases or two different portfolios.

Where do the investment portfolios come from?

The portfolios are constructed and calculated by Neptoon Wealth using an investment universe that includes investment funds offered by European banks, publicly traded stocks, ETFs and bonds. They represent different investment strategies and combinations of expected returns, risk and costs. The purpose is to let you choose your own investment assumptions and explore their impact on Economic Balance Day, wealth development, future consumption and Legacy.

Why must I choose a portfolio for both Phase 1 and Phase 2?

Because the two phases serve different economic purposes. In Phase 1, regular savings contributions are added to the Free Investment Portfolio, and wealth is accumulated towards Economic Balance Day. In Phase 2, economic resources are used to finance your desired consumption and other obligations. You therefore select a separate risk and return profile for each phase. Neptoon uses the selected portfolio's expected net return as a central assumption for that phase.

Does Neptoon automatically select the best investment portfolio?

No. Neptoon does not optimize your investment portfolio selection or determine how much investment risk you should take. You independently choose a portfolio for each phase from the available investment models. Neptoon then calculates the earliest feasible Economic Balance Day under your selected economic assumptions. It is the timing of Economic Balance Day that is calculated, not an optimal investment strategy.

What does risk level 1–7 mean?

Neptoon uses a common risk scale from 1 to 7, where higher levels represent greater estimated investment risk. The scale covers both diversified portfolios and concentrated individual investments. Level 7 therefore does not necessarily represent an ordinary diversified equity portfolio. The risk level is calculated from the portfolio's estimated risk and serves as a descriptive indicator, not as an independent decision variable in the Life Cycle model.

Why do diversified model portfolios typically only reach risk level 5 out of 7?

Because Neptoon's risk scale also includes investments without diversification. The available model portfolios contain multiple underlying investments and therefore typically have lower overall risk than highly concentrated investments. Approximately 5/7 represents the highest risk among the available diversified model portfolios. This does not mean that Neptoon's risk scale ends at 5. A portfolio invested 100% in a single stock may, for example, receive a risk level of 7/7 because of substantial company-specific risk.

Is risk level 5/7 the highest risk I can choose?

It is approximately the upper risk limit among the available diversified model portfolios in Life Cycle. The full risk scale still extends to 7/7 and also covers concentrated investments, such as a portfolio consisting of a single stock. Neptoon therefore does not limit the risk scale itself to 5. Rather, the diversification of the available model portfolios means that their estimated risk normally remains below the scale's maximum.

Is risk level 7/7 necessarily better than 5/7?

No. A higher risk level indicates greater estimated investment risk, not necessarily a better expected return or a more suitable strategy. An investment concentrated in a single stock may carry substantial company-specific risk without necessarily offering a correspondingly higher expected return. Diversification can reduce this risk without necessarily reducing expected returns to the same extent. Neptoon therefore does not recommend pursuing the highest possible number on the risk scale.

Can I choose different risk levels for the two phases?

Yes. You select an independent investment portfolio for each phase and can therefore choose different risk and return profiles. For example, you might select a more aggressive portfolio during wealth accumulation and a more defensive portfolio during wealth consumption, or vice versa. Neptoon calculates the consequences of your choices but does not prescribe which combination you should use.

Does the risk level determine the portfolio's expected return?

No. Expected return is calculated from the selected portfolio's underlying investments, their weights and Neptoon's economic assumptions. The risk level is a separate indicator of the portfolio's estimated risk. Two portfolios can therefore have the same risk level but different expected returns and costs. The Life Cycle calculation uses the selected portfolio's expected return assumption, not the numerical risk level, to determine investment returns.

Are the portfolios actual investment products?

No. Neptoon's model portfolios are analytical combinations of underlying investment instruments. They are not standalone investment products that can be purchased directly. The underlying funds, ETFs and stocks may, however, be actual listed or distributed investment products. Neptoon does not open investment accounts or execute trades on your behalf.

Are the banks' model portfolios official bank products?

No. The bank-related portfolios are Neptoon Wealth's own model representations of investment profiles and risk levels. They are not official portfolios, investment recommendations or products issued or approved by the named banks. Their composition, expected returns, risk and costs may differ from the banks' actual investment solutions.

What does a bank's name mean in the portfolio overview?

A bank's name identifies a model reference to an investment profile or investment universe. It does not mean that the bank constructed, sponsored, approved or distributed Neptoon's model portfolio. Neptoon Wealth is responsible for the model's composition and calculation assumptions.

What does 'Issuer' mean in the portfolio overview?

'Issuer' identifies the issuer or manager of an underlying investment instrument, such as an investment fund or ETF. This is not necessarily the bank associated with the model portfolio's investment profile. A model portfolio may contain instruments from several different issuers.

Who manages the individual investment funds?

The underlying investment funds and ETFs are issued and managed by their respective financial providers. Neptoon Wealth uses these instruments as building blocks in its model portfolios. The issuers are responsible for their own products, but not for Neptoon's portfolio weights, economic assumptions or calculated Life Cycle results.

How does Neptoon construct its model portfolios?

Neptoon Wealth constructs model portfolios based on different investment strategies, asset classes and risk profiles. Each portfolio has a defined allocation across underlying investment instruments. Neptoon then calculates expected returns, risk and costs based on the instruments' characteristics, portfolio weights and the economic assumptions applied.

What does diversification mean?

Diversification means spreading investments across several companies, asset classes, sectors or markets rather than concentrating them in a single investment. One purpose is to reduce company-specific risk. A diversified portfolio may therefore have lower overall risk than an investment in a single stock, even if both have substantial exposure to equity markets. However, diversification cannot eliminate general market risk.

Is a model portfolio automatically well diversified?

Not necessarily. Diversification depends on the composition, weights and relationships between the underlying investments. Holding several funds does not automatically improve diversification if they invest in the same companies or markets. Neptoon estimates the portfolio's overall risk, but even a broadly diversified portfolio can experience substantial losses.

Why do the portfolios have different risk levels?

Different combinations of stocks, bonds and other investments produce different expected returns and risks. A higher equity allocation will often increase estimated risk, while a larger bond allocation typically produces a more defensive profile. However, actual risk also depends on the characteristics and relationships of the underlying investments. Neptoon's risk level is a calculated indicator, not a guarantee of future price fluctuations.

How are the portfolios' expected returns calculated?

Neptoon calculates expected returns using forward-looking return assumptions for the underlying investments and their respective portfolio weights. Investment costs are included when calculating expected net returns. Expected return is a modeling assumption, not a historical average, a guaranteed return or a return promised by a bank.

How are investment costs included?

Neptoon includes estimated ongoing costs of the underlying investment instruments when calculating expected net returns. Actual investment expenses may also include trading costs, custody fees, advisory fees and currency conversion charges, which are not necessarily fully reflected in the model.

How are portfolio returns used in Life Cycle?

Each of the two selected portfolios has its own expected return assumption. Neptoon applies the relevant expected return, adjusted for the model's tax and inflation assumptions, when calculating wealth development during the corresponding phase. The return assumption also serves as the financing rate on any negative balance in the Free Investment Portfolio. This is a symmetric modeling assumption, not a guarantee that actual borrowing will be available on equivalent terms.

Should I choose a more defensive portfolio in Phase 2?

Not necessarily. The choice depends on factors such as your other assets, expected remaining lifetime, desired consumption, Legacy and willingness to accept investment risk. A more defensive portfolio may reduce certain risks but may also reduce expected returns. Neptoon allows you to explore the consequences of different choices without recommending any particular investment strategy.

Are the portfolios' historical returns a guarantee of future performance?

No. Historical returns illustrate past market developments and can help explain investment risk. They do not guarantee future results. Neptoon's Life Cycle calculations are based on forward-looking expected returns rather than an assumption that historical performance will repeat itself.

Do Neptoon's model portfolios constitute personalized investment advice?

No. The model portfolios are used for financial analysis, education and illustration of economic relationships. Neptoon does not perform an individual suitability assessment or recommend buying or selling particular securities through Life Cycle. Your portfolio selections in Phase 1 and Phase 2 are independent modeling assumptions, not personalized investment recommendations.

How does investment risk affect uncertainty around my Economic Balance Day?

Neptoon calculates an expected Economic Balance Day (n*) based on your selected economic assumptions and investment portfolios in Phase 1 and Phase 2. However, investment returns are uncertain. Neptoon therefore applies a stochastic uncertainty calculation that incorporates the risks of both selected portfolios. The result is presented as an uncertainty interval around the expected Economic Balance Day. An interval of five years, for example, illustrates how different possible return paths may lead to an earlier or later Economic Balance Day. The interval is not a guarantee, but a model-based representation of investment uncertainty.

Why does the uncertainty interval become wider when I choose riskier portfolios?

Because greater investment risk creates greater uncertainty about future wealth development. Neptoon incorporates the risks of both the Phase 1 portfolio, where wealth is accumulated, and the Phase 2 portfolio, where wealth is used to finance consumption and Legacy. Risk in Phase 1 affects uncertainty about the wealth accumulated before Economic Balance Day, while risk in Phase 2 affects uncertainty about the portfolio's ability to finance future consumption. The combined stochastic calculation translates these risks into an uncertainty interval around n*. Two different portfolio combinations may therefore produce the same expected Economic Balance Day but different uncertainty intervals.
