Debt, loans, interest and repayments

Should I register my mortgage separately from my home?

Yes. Enter the property's full market value under Real Estate and the remaining mortgage debt under Loans and Liabilities. Neptoon records the property as an asset and the mortgage as a separate liability. The difference between these two values represents your economic home equity.

What is the difference between a mortgage and a bank loan?

In Neptoon, the mortgage is directly linked to the registered property and is automatically settled when the property is realized. A bank loan, by contrast, is treated as an independent liability that follows its own repayment schedule, regardless of whether the home is sold. Both loans have separate assumptions for outstanding debt, interest and principal repayments.

Can I include a bank loan taken out for consumption?

Yes. A bank loan can represent debt that is not directly linked to the registered property, such as a car loan, personal loan or consumer loan. You enter the outstanding balance, interest rate and repayment terms. Neptoon then calculates the ongoing interest and principal repayments and includes them in your overall economic Life Cycle.

How does Neptoon handle interest and principal repayments on my loans?

Neptoon continuously calculates interest and principal repayments on registered loans. All loan payments are handled through the Free Investment Portfolio, which serves as the model's central financing account in both Phase 1 and Phase 2. Interest and financing costs reduce your total economic wealth. Principal repayments, on the other hand, reduce outstanding debt and therefore improve net wealth on the liability side of the Balance Sheet, but are simultaneously financed through the Free Investment Portfolio. The model records both sides of the transaction.

Does repaying debt increase my net wealth?

A principal repayment improves your net wealth in isolation because the outstanding liability becomes smaller. However, the repayment must also be financed. In Neptoon, the payment is deducted from the Free Investment Portfolio while the outstanding debt is reduced by the same amount. Total net wealth therefore does not change solely as a result of the repayment. Principal repayment is a Balance Sheet reallocation, while interest and other financing expenses are genuine economic costs.

Should I include loan payments in my monthly savings or consumption?

No. Your monthly savings contribution in Phase 1 is the independent amount you choose to add to the Free Investment Portfolio. Your consumption target in Phase 2 represents your desired ongoing standard of living after tax. Interest and principal repayments on registered loans are calculated and financed separately through the Free Investment Portfolio in both phases. If you also include these payments in your savings or consumption target, you risk counting the same payments twice.

Can Neptoon finance loan payments even if my current income is insufficient?

Yes, within the model's economic calculation. The Free Investment Portfolio serves as a central financing account that can draw on existing and future economic resources to finance interest, principal repayments and other modeled payments. The account may also have a negative balance. In that case, Neptoon applies the expected return of the selected investment portfolio as the financing interest rate on the negative balance. This is a simplified modeling assumption, not a guarantee that a bank will provide equivalent credit facilities.

What happens to my mortgage when I sell my home?

When the registered property is realized, the remaining mortgage debt is settled directly from the property's calculated sale proceeds, together with any deferred property capital gains tax. Only the net proceeds are transferred to the Free Investment Portfolio, regardless of whether the sale takes place during Phase 1 or Phase 2. The property and settled mortgage debt are simultaneously removed from the Balance Sheet, and future mortgage payments cease.

What happens to my bank loan if I sell my home?

In Neptoon, the bank loan is independent of the property's realization. Selling your home therefore does not automatically settle the bank loan. The loan continues according to its own repayment schedule, and the remaining interest and principal repayments continue to be financed through the Free Investment Portfolio. This also applies if the bank loan was originally taken out in connection with purchasing the home.

Why does Neptoon include deferred tax as a liability?

Certain assets, such as private businesses and private pensions, may have associated tax liabilities that have not yet been paid. Neptoon records these deferred taxes separately on the liability side of the Balance Sheet. Net wealth therefore reflects both the asset's gross value and the expected future tax obligation. When the asset is realized, the tax is settled through the net proceeds, and the corresponding tax liability is removed from the Balance Sheet to prevent double counting.

What is the difference between principal repayments and interest expenses?

Principal repayment is the repayment of the original loan amount. It reduces outstanding debt and improves net wealth on the Balance Sheet in isolation, but simultaneously requires an equivalent payment from the Free Investment Portfolio. Interest, by contrast, is the cost of financing and reduces total economic wealth. Neptoon distinguishes between these two components because they have different economic effects, even though both result in payments from the Free Investment Portfolio.

Can I leave negative net wealth at the end of my life?

No. Neptoon is designed with a terminal constraint requiring your total economic net wealth at the selected end-of-life age to be at least zero. This means the model does not accept a Life Cycle in which your remaining debt and other liabilities exceed the value of your remaining assets. If you have specified a positive Legacy target, that target must also be satisfied. Neptoon calculates Economic Balance Day subject to this terminal constraint. This is a mathematical assumption of the model, not a guarantee that your actual finances cannot end with negative net wealth.
