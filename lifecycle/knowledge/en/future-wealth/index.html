Inheritance and future wealth

Understand how expected inheritance and other future resources contribute to wealth.

Can I include an expected inheritance?

Yes. You can enter an expected future inheritance, including its economic value today, its expected value when received and the age at which you expect to receive it. Neptoon includes the inheritance's calculated economic value on your Balance Sheet even before it is received, but the amount only becomes available in the Free Investment Portfolio at the specified realization age. The expected value should be net of any inheritance taxes and associated costs. An expected inheritance is naturally uncertain and is not a guaranteed future payment.

What other future values can I include?

You can include identifiable economic assets or amounts that are expected to be realized at a later date. Examples include gifts, compensation payments, Danish Share Savings Accounts (ASK), retirement savings accounts (aldersopsparing), holiday homes, additional properties, ownership interests in businesses or valuable personal assets. You enter their current value, expected future value and realization age. The model treats these items as separate economic resources that are subsequently transferred to the Free Investment Portfolio.

How do I include a Danish Share Savings Account (ASK)?

A Danish Share Savings Account (Aktiesparekonto or ASK) represents existing financial wealth rather than future income in itself. You can include its current value and expected value at a future realization date under Other Future Values. Investment returns in a Danish ASK are generally subject to annual mark-to-market taxation at 17%. The expected future value must therefore already reflect this ongoing taxation. Neptoon does not separately calculate ASK taxation or the account's specific rules, and the same tax should not be deducted again upon realization.

How do I include a Danish retirement savings account (aldersopsparing)?

A Danish retirement savings account (aldersopsparing) can be included as a separate future value, with its current value, expected value upon withdrawal and selected realization age. Investment returns are generally subject to ongoing PAL taxation at 15.3%, while withdrawals are normally tax-free. The expected future value must therefore be net of ongoing investment return taxation. Neptoon does not separately calculate contribution limits, withdrawal conditions or other specific pension rules applicable to aldersopsparing.

What is the difference between mark-to-market taxation and deferred tax?

Under mark-to-market taxation, investment returns are taxed on an ongoing basis, even without a sale. Under realization-based taxation, tax is generally settled only when a gain is realized. Under Other Future Values, you are responsible for ensuring that the expected future value is stated net of relevant taxes and costs. Neptoon does not automatically accumulate separate tax liabilities for these items. A future value that is already net of tax must therefore not be taxed again when transferred to the Free Investment Portfolio.

How does Neptoon calculate the implied return?

When you enter both an economic value today and an expected future value, Neptoon calculates the annual implied return connecting the two amounts over the specified period. The implied return is a calculated growth rate, not an independent return forecast provided by Neptoon. Both values are required for the calculation. It is important that the values are stated consistently with respect to taxes, costs and the selected realization age.

Why can't I enter all my personal belongings?

Neptoon is an economic Life Cycle model, not a personal bookkeeping system. Ordinary belongings such as furniture, electronics, clothing and other household items are generally considered consumption goods and are not automatically included. The model focuses on economic resources that can make a meaningful contribution to future consumption or Legacy. However, valuable personal assets with expected sale proceeds can be registered under Other Future Values.

What if I own something valuable that I expect to sell?

If you own a valuable asset, such as a holiday home, boat, collection or another marketable asset, you can include its expected net proceeds under Other Future Values. Enter the asset's current value, expected future value after any outstanding debt, taxes and selling costs, and the age at which you expect to realize it. Neptoon does not separately calculate financing, ongoing operating expenses or taxes for these additional assets.

What happens when I receive or realize a future amount?

At the specified realization age, the calculated future net value is transferred to the Free Investment Portfolio, regardless of whether realization occurs during Phase 1 or Phase 2. The amount then earns the return of your selected investment portfolio for the relevant phase. Before realization, the item is already included as an economic resource on your Balance Sheet. Realization therefore represents a conversion from a future economic value into available investment capital, rather than a new savings contribution. The same value must not be counted twice.
